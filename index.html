01 / The Transition Narrative

From logistics software
to AI-native platform.

The real PMM challenge at Locus isn't writing good copy. It's helping enterprise buyers understand a category that didn't exist three years ago.

Locus was positioned as
A last-mile route optimization tool. Smart routing, delivery tracking, driver dispatch. Category: logistics SaaS. Buyers: ops managers buying point solutions.
→
Locus now needs to say
An Agentic TMS that spans planning, execution, and settlement. Category: AI-native logistics platform. Buyers: VP Supply Chain, CIO, Head of Logistics buying strategic infrastructure.
Proof points looked like
Faster routes. Fewer failed deliveries. Driver app ratings.
→
Proof points need to be
12M+ automated decisions per day. 250+ real-world constraints. $320M verified cost savings. 99.5% SLA adherence at enterprise scale.
Old narrative hook
"Optimize your last mile."
→
New narrative hook
"Your logistics network runs on decisions. Agentic TMS makes 12 million of them a day."
The Opportunity

Why this transition matters now

Ingka Group's acquisition in October 2025 isn't just a funding event. It's a credibility signal: IKEA runs arguably the world's most scrutinized home delivery operation, and they chose Locus. That proof point needs to be woven into every enterprise pitch.

Meanwhile, the category is forming in real time. QKS named Locus a TMS leader in 2025. Gartner has flagged Locus for five consecutive years. The window to own "Agentic TMS" as a category is open, but won't stay open.

The PMM Gap

What the story needs to do

  • Reframe Locus from "logistics tool" to "decision infrastructure" without confusing existing customers
  • Make agentic AI legible to supply chain buyers who are skeptical of AI hype but open to provable outcomes
  • Differentiate from Blue Yonder, Oracle TMS, SAP TM on architecture, not just features
  • Use the IKEA signal strategically, not just as a logo placement
  • Build a buyer-specific narrative for Head of Logistics vs. VP Supply Chain vs. CIO
Positioning Premise

The core thesis I'd build from

Enterprise logistics has always been a decision problem, not a visibility problem. Traditional TMS platforms gave you better dashboards. Agentic TMS gives you better decisions, at machine speed, with human guardrails. Locus doesn't just show you what's happening. It acts on it.

That's the fundamental shift. And framed that way, legacy platforms can't compete by adding AI features. The architecture is different at the root. Locus needs to own that distinction before SAP and Blue Yonder start using the word "agentic" on their marketing pages.

02 / Positioning Audit

Where the current
messaging holds and leaks.

Working

The "promises become proof" anchor line

Strong directional statement. Operationally credible. Avoids abstract AI language and speaks to SLA accountability, which is the #1 pain for logistics heads at enterprise scale.

Working

Proof metrics front and center

The 20% cost reduction and 66% faster planning cycles figures are specific enough to trigger credibility. Most SaaS companies hide numbers like these in case studies.

Weak

No clear hero claim for the AI-native transition

"Agentic TMS" appears in the description but isn't anchored to a specific capability story. A first-time buyer doesn't leave understanding what makes Locus architecturally different from a TMS that "uses AI."

Weak

The IKEA signal is underleveraged

IKEA runs 500M+ customer deliveries globally. Ingka Group acquiring Locus is a massive proof point for enterprise credibility. It deserves more than a mention. It should anchor the trust narrative.

Opportunity

Add a "what makes this different" explainer module

A short interactive section explaining: Legacy TMS plans. Agentic TMS decides. Could use the "12M decisions/day" stat as the anchor. Enterprise buyers need to understand why architecture matters, not just outcomes.

Opportunity

Segment entry points by buyer role

Head of Logistics, VP Supply Chain, and CIO have very different questions on arrival. A role-aware navigation or content branch could increase qualified engagement and accelerate sales conversations.

→

Overall verdict: The homepage reads like a logistics platform that has AI. The goal is to read like an AI platform that runs logistics. That's a meaningful rewrite, not a tweak, and it starts with the hero section and carries through every product description.

Working

Gartner recognition used consistently

Five consecutive years of Gartner recognition is strong third-party validation. It reduces skepticism at first contact and gives procurement teams an easy reference point.

Working

QKS TMS Leader positioning in 2025

Being named a category leader in the QKS SPARK Matrix for TMS is a defensible claim. It directly supports the "Agentic TMS" category narrative if used in context.

Gap

"Agentic TMS" risks becoming empty jargon

The category name is claimed, but the proof of what agentism means operationally is scattered. Buyers need a concrete model: here's a decision that used to require human intervention, here's how Locus handles it autonomously.

Watch

Competitors will start using "agentic" soon

Blue Yonder and SAP have the resources to rebrand features as "agentic." The window to own the word with proof is now. Needs content moats: detailed explainers, customer stories, technical depth that legacy vendors can't replicate quickly.

→

Category play verdict: Locus is in the right position to own "Agentic TMS." The infrastructure for the claim exists: genuine architecture, real deployments, verified outcomes. The gap is narrative scaffolding, the layer between "we are agentic" and "here's specifically what that means for your operation."

Working

Founder-led thought leadership

Nishith Rastogi publishing on logistics strategy and supply chain decision systems gives Locus a credible voice. Founder POV content tends to outperform brand content in B2B at this stage.

Gap

Content is informational, not POV-driven

The blog covers TMS comparisons, feature explainers, logistics guides. That generates SEO traffic but doesn't build category conviction. Locus needs content that argues a point, not just explains one.

Opportunity

Customer data narratives are largely untapped

With 1.5B+ deliveries and 350+ enterprise deployments, Locus has more operational intelligence than any competitor. Turning that data into original research and benchmarks would be defensible, hard to copy, and category-building.

Opportunity

The "human governance" angle is underdeveloped

One of the most important objections to agentic AI is "what if it's wrong?" Locus has guardrails and human oversight built into the platform. That feature deserves its own content narrative aimed squarely at risk-conscious CIOs.

→

Content verdict: The content strategy should pivot from informational coverage to original intelligence. Locus has the deployment data to publish logistics benchmarks no competitor can replicate. That becomes a category-defining content moat.

03 / GTM Brief

How I'd take the
Agentic TMS story to market.

Core Narrative

One sentence the company should own

"Your logistics network runs on millions of decisions a day. Locus is the only platform built to make them."

This works because it reframes the buyer's problem (decision volume, not route planning), makes the uniqueness claim structural (not feature-level), and opens the door for proof: 12M decisions/day, 250+ constraints, 99.5% SLA.

The Foil

What we position against

The category foil isn't Blue Yonder or SAP directly. It's the entire legacy TMS model: systems that help humans plan better, versus systems that plan and act autonomously.

This framing lets Locus win on architecture, not feature checklist. You can't beat Oracle on breadth. You can beat them on the speed and intelligence of autonomous decisioning.

Launch Phasing

90-day narrative build for the Agentic TMS story

01
Days 1–30: Foundation
Lock the narrative and build the asset stack
Rewrite the homepage hero section and product pages around the decision-intelligence frame. Produce a single flagship asset: "The Agentic TMS Explainer", a visual piece that shows a logistics scenario playing out in a traditional TMS vs. Locus. Build a buyer-specific landing page for each of the three personas (Logistics Head, VP SC, CIO). Create internal-facing materials so Sales can start using the new language immediately.
02
Days 31–60: Proof Layer
Anchor claims with customer stories and data
Source two flagship customer stories from different verticals (one FMCG, one retail). Each story should lead with a decision scenario: here is the specific logistics problem, here is what an agentic decision looks like versus a human-assisted one, here is the outcome. Produce an original benchmarks report using anonymized platform data: "State of Logistics Decision-Making 2025." Position Locus as the definitive source.
03
Days 61–90: Amplification
Push the story into market through events, content, and partner channels
Take the narrative to supply chain events and trade press. Activate the IKEA/Ingka endorsement in a co-authored piece on logistics transformation. Run a targeted ABM campaign against the top 50 enterprise accounts in retail and FMCG. Build co-marketing angles with the 1,000+ pre-integrated carriers, turning them into distribution partners for Locus content.
ICP Prioritization

Where to focus first

  • FMCG/CPG enterprises in Southeast Asia and the Middle East: high delivery density, multi-carrier complexity, proven Locus deployment history
  • Mid-size retail chains looking to move off legacy TMS platforms ahead of peak season planning cycles
  • 3PLs expanding geographically: they need a system that works across carrier networks and countries without a 12-month implementation
Sales Enablement

What the field actually needs

  • A one-page "Agentic TMS vs. Legacy TMS" comparison that sales reps can use in discovery calls
  • Vertical-specific ROI calculators with conservative, moderate, and aggressive scenarios
  • An objection handler specifically for "we're already on SAP/Blue Yonder": focus on modular deployment and coexistence, not rip-and-replace
  • A reference story for each major vertical, organized by the buyer's pain first

The Locus sale typically involves three distinct decision-makers. Each arrives with a different question. The PMM's job is to make sure Locus answers each one before sales gets in the room.

Head of Logistics / Ops Director

The person who lives inside the daily exceptions.

Their mornings start with: which carrier failed overnight, which SLAs are at risk, which routes need manual replanning. They've seen "AI" tools before and most of them just gave them a better dashboard to manage the same problem. They don't want to be impressed. They want to not be woken up at 6am.

What they actually ask:
"How does this reduce the number of exceptions my team handles manually? And what happens when the system gets it wrong?"
The message that lands:
Locus surfaces risk early and recommends the next best move before the exception becomes a missed SLA. Your team shifts from reactive firefighting to exception oversight. The system handles the 95%, your team calls the 5% that need judgment.
Proof point to lead with:
99.5% SLA adherence ratio across 350+ enterprise deployments. Not pilot conditions. Production scale.
VP Supply Chain / SVP Logistics

The executive who's accountable when the network breaks.

They're measured on cost, service levels, and strategic flexibility. They're looking at a 3-year planning horizon while also managing quarter-to-quarter pressure. They want to know if Locus is a tactical tool or a strategic one. They're thinking about what the network looks like after the transition, not just the software itself.

What they actually ask:
"Does this reduce our dependency on carrier relationships and manual planning, or does it just make it faster?"
The message that lands:
Locus unifies orders, capacity, and carrier networks into a single living plan. Over time, the platform learns your network's patterns and starts recommending structural changes, not just tactical optimizations.
Proof point to lead with:
$320M in verified transit cost savings and 20% average cost reduction. Frame it as network ROI, not per-route savings.
CIO / VP Technology

The person who will be blamed if the AI makes a bad call.

They've approved a lot of AI implementations that didn't survive contact with production. They want to know: is this a black box, can it coexist with SAP/Oracle, what does the failure mode look like, and who's accountable when it goes wrong? They're not against AI. They're against uncontrolled AI.

What they actually ask:
"What are the human guardrails? Does this require ripping out existing systems?"
The message that lands:
Locus runs with embedded human governance: AI co-pilots with guardrails, not replacements. The system flags decisions for human review when confidence is below threshold. API-first architecture with pre-built connectors for SAP, Oracle, and Microsoft means no rip-and-replace.
Proof point to lead with:
Battle-tested in 350+ deployments across 30+ countries. Ingka Group (IKEA) chose Locus as the infrastructure for their global delivery operation. If it's good enough for IKEA's compliance requirements, it's good enough for yours.

Sales Enablement / Discovery Brief

Discovery call framing: The Decision Audit

Opening question: "On a typical day, how many logistics decisions does your team make that could theoretically be automated?"

[Wait for number. Most ops leaders underestimate by 10x.]

Follow-up: "The enterprises we work with typically have between 50,000 and 500,000 such decisions per day: routing exceptions, carrier allocation, SLA risk flags, dispatch adjustments. Most of them are being made manually or not at all. What does that cost you in delayed responses and SLA misses?"

Pivot to Locus: "What Locus does is take that decision load off your team systematically. Not by giving you a better dashboard to make the decisions yourself. By making the decisions autonomously, with clear escalation rules for the edge cases that need a human."

Objection handling (SAP/Blue Yonder): "We don't require replacing anything. Locus runs alongside your existing ERP and WMS through pre-built connectors. The way most customers start is picking one decision category, carrier allocation is common, running Locus on it for 60 days, and measuring the delta before expanding scope."

Why this works: The "Decision Audit" framing turns discovery into a diagnostic exercise the buyer finds genuinely useful. It surfaces their actual problem, creates a quantification anchor, and positions Locus as the solution before the product demo even starts.
